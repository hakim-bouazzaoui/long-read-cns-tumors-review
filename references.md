# Curated references

All entries below are final journal publications. Preprints and conference-only abstracts are excluded.

## Diagnostic framework / methylome clinical utility

1. **Capper D, et al. (2018).** DNA methylation-based classification of central nervous system tumours. *Nature* 555:469–474. DOI: `10.1038/nature26000`  
   Foundational Heidelberg methylation classifier and large external validation.

2. **Louis DN, et al. (2021).** The 2021 WHO Classification of Tumors of the Central Nervous System: a summary. *Neuro-Oncology* 23:1231–1251. DOI: `10.1093/neuonc/noab106`  
   WHO CNS5 integrated histomolecular framework.

3. **Wu Z, et al. (2022).** Impact of the methylation classifier and ancillary methods on CNS tumor diagnostics. *Neuro-Oncology* 24:571–581. DOI: `10.1093/neuonc/noab227`  
   Large challenging-diagnosis cohort; documents diagnostic impact and the effect of tumour purity.

4. **Sahm F, et al. (2023).** Molecular diagnostic tools for the WHO 2021 classification of gliomas, glioneuronal and neuronal tumors; an EANO guideline. *Neuro-Oncology* 25:1731–1749. DOI: `10.1093/neuonc/noad100`  
   Formal EANO guidance on methylome profiling and complementary molecular assays.

5. **Galbraith K, et al. (2023).** Clinical utility of whole-genome DNA methylation profiling as a primary molecular diagnostic assay for CNS tumors. *Neuro-Oncology Advances* 5:vdad076. DOI: `10.1093/noajnl/vdad076`  
   Prospective 1,921-tumour clinical utility study.

6. **Aldape K, et al. (2025).** cIMPACT-NOW update 9: Recommendations on utilization of genome-wide DNA methylation profiling for CNS tumor diagnostics. *Neuro-Oncology Advances* 7:vdae228. DOI: `10.1093/noajnl/vdae228`  
   Recent consensus recommendations specifically addressing genome-wide methylation profiling.

## Long-read / nanopore CNS tumour diagnostics

7. **Wongsurawat T, et al. (2020).** A novel Cas9-targeted long-read assay for simultaneous detection of IDH1/2 mutations and clinically relevant MGMT methylation in fresh biopsies of diffuse glioma. *Acta Neuropathologica Communications* 8:87. DOI: `10.1186/s40478-020-00963-0`  
   Fresh-biopsy targeted long-read proof-of-feasibility for SNVs plus methylation.

8. **Djirackor L, et al. (2021).** Intraoperative DNA methylation classification of brain tumors impacts neurosurgical strategy. *Neuro-Oncology Advances* 3:vdab149. DOI: `10.1093/noajnl/vdab149`  
   Mixed adult/paediatric cohort; 55 adults overall. In the 20 intraoperative cases, molecular classification would have supported modification of surgical strategy in 12/20, although this decision-impact subset was not stratified by age.

9. **Patel A, et al. (2022).** Rapid-CNS2: rapid comprehensive adaptive nanopore-sequencing of CNS tumors, a proof-of-concept study. *Acta Neuropathologica* 143:609–612. DOI: `10.1007/s00401-022-02415-6`  
   Adaptive sampling for methylation class, CNV, SNV/indels and MGMT from fresh/cryopreserved material.

10. **Kuschel LP, et al. (2023).** Robust methylation-based classification of brain tumours using nanopore sequencing. *Neuropathology and Applied Neurobiology* 49:e12856. DOI: `10.1111/nan.12856`  
    nanoDx / sparse methylation classification across tumour types.

11. **Vermeulen C, et al. (2023).** Ultra-fast deep-learned CNS tumour classification during surgery. *Nature* 622:842–849. DOI: `10.1038/s41586-023-06615-2`  
    Sturgeon; deep-learning classification from extremely sparse nanopore methylomes during surgery.

12. **Emiliani FE, et al. (2025).** Nanopore-based random genomic sampling for intraoperative molecular diagnosis. *Genome Medicine* 17:6. DOI: `10.1186/s13073-025-01427-7`  
    iSCORED; prospective 15-case intraoperative cohort with 8 high-grade gliomas, 4 low-grade gliomas and 3 spindle-cell neoplasms. ~105-min workflow producing methylation classification plus genome-wide CNV and focal oncogene amplification information.

13. **Brändl B, et al. (2025).** Rapid brain tumor classification from sparse epigenomic data. *Nature Medicine* 31:840–848. DOI: `10.1038/s41591-024-03435-3`  
    MethyLYZR; Bayesian streaming methylation classification with very short sequencing times.

14. **Patel A, et al. (2025).** Prospective, multicenter validation of a platform for rapid molecular profiling of central nervous system tumors. *Nature Medicine* 31:1567–1577. DOI: `10.1038/s41591-025-03562-5`  
    Multicenter Rapid-CNS2 validation; in 18 true intraoperative workflows, methylation + CNV provided clinically relevant additional information in 13/18 (72.2%), but results were not yet used to drive surgery.

15. **Yuan D, et al. (2025).** crossNN is an explainable framework for cross-platform DNA methylation-based classification of tumors. *Nature Cancer* 6:1283–1294. DOI: `10.1038/s43018-025-00976-5`  
    Platform-agnostic sparse-methylome classifier validated on nanopore and other platforms.

16. **Deacon S, et al. (2025).** ROBIN: A unified nanopore-based assay integrating intraoperative methylome classification and next-day comprehensive profiling for ultra-rapid tumor diagnosis. *Neuro-Oncology* 27:2035–2046. DOI: `10.1093/neuonc/noaf103`  
    Integrated real-time methylation classification plus CNV/SNV/SV/fusion/MGMT workflow. Adult-relevant entities include glioblastoma, astrocytoma, oligodendroglioma and meningioma; not an adult-specific decision-impact trial.

17. **Filser M, et al. (2025).** Nanopore sequencing as a cutting-edge technology for medulloblastoma classification. *Neuro-Oncology* 27:1313–1324. DOI: `10.1093/neuonc/noae279`  
    **Montpellier-relevant experience:** Valérie Rigau and Gilles Palenzuela (CHU Montpellier) are co-authors. On 44 frozen medulloblastomas benchmarked against EPIC arrays, 42/44 (95.5%) were correctly subgrouped; in the 116-case integrated-diagnosis cohort, 106/116 (91.4%) were correctly subgrouped. In a Flongle subset, 17/18 (94.4%) were correctly classified, and 28/30 (93.3%) were correctly subtyped.

18. **Hu W, et al. (2026).** Nanopore-based DNA methylation profiling for rapid molecular classification of NOS/NEC CNS tumors: multi-institutional evaluation using FFPE archives and frozen tissues. *Acta Neuropathologica Communications*. DOI: `10.1186/s40478-026-02359-y`  
    Recent multi-institutional validation including frozen-tissue samples and diagnostically difficult NOS/NEC cases.

19. **Eelkman Rooda OHJ, et al. (2026).** The emerging role of intraoperative nanopore sequencing on the neurosurgical strategy in paediatric embryonal brain tumours. *Child's Nervous System* 42:282. DOI: `10.1007/s00381-026-07377-8`  
    Peer-reviewed review focused on biology-informed extent-of-resection decisions and prospective endpoints including morbidity and second-look surgery.

20. **Sie M, et al. (2026).** How 'Sturgeon' guides the surgeon in pediatric neuro-oncology. *Neuro-Oncology*. DOI: `10.1093/neuonc/noag187`; PMID `42576322`.  
    **Current strongest direct nanopore clinical-impact study.** 94 consecutive paediatric patients; 82/94 (87.2%) correct diagnoses in <90 min. When informative, Sturgeon supported the intended strategy in 85.7% and **actually changed surgical strategy in 14.3%**, toward either more aggressive or more limited resection. Published 10 August 2026; no longer a preprint.

## Adult diffuse-glioma / surgical-impact rationale

21. **Drexler R, et al. (2023).** DNA methylation subclasses predict the benefit from gross total tumor resection in IDH-wildtype glioblastoma patients. *Neuro-Oncology* 25:315–325. DOI: `10.1093/neuonc/noac177`; PMID `35868257`.  
    Multicentre methylation study of 430 newly diagnosed IDH-wildtype glioblastomas. Maximized EOR was associated with better OS/PFS in RTK I and RTK II, but not in the MES methylation subclass. Strong biological rationale for the hypothesis that the benefit/risk balance of aggressive resection may not be uniform across molecular subtypes.

22. **Drexler R, Lim M, Hervey-Jumper SL. (2025).** Molecular-based decision-making in glioblastoma surgery: When to aim for supramaximal resection. *Neuro-Oncology* 27:1434–1442. DOI: `10.1093/neuonc/noaf062`; PMID `40037513`.  
    Adult glioblastoma review explicitly developing the concept of biology-informed surgery and discussing intraoperative nanopore methylation classification as a future route to molecularly guided resection decisions.

23. **Wu J, et al. (2025).** Rapid diagnosis of adult-type diffuse glioma using a layered scheme. *BMC Medicine* 23:325. DOI: `10.1186/s12916-025-04124-9`; PMID `40457320`.  
    Adult-only prospective multicentre molecular-diagnosis study: 296 prospective patients, ~35-min rapid IDH/TERTp testing. **40 patients** initially called lower-grade glioma on frozen section were molecularly reclassified toward glioblastoma; **20 patients** initially called gliosis were correctly recognized as diffuse glioma. Not nanopore, but strong evidence that intraoperative molecular information can correct surgically relevant diagnostic uncertainty in adults. The paper also highlights the remaining need for rapid `CDKN2A/B`, `EGFR`, `+7/-10` and other second-layer information.

24. **Gerritsen JKW, et al.; PIONEER Consortium / RANO resect. (2026).** A comprehensive framework for glioma surgery, part 1: intraoperative recommendations for mapping, monitoring, and decision making. *The Lancet Oncology* 27:e11–e23. DOI: `10.1016/S1470-2045(25)00531-5`; PMID `41449153`.  
    Adult diffuse-glioma policy review: more extensive resection is associated with longer PFS/OS, but must be balanced against preservation of neurological function. Explicitly discusses emerging intraoperative diagnostics as potential modifiers of surgical strategy.

25. **Gerritsen JKW, et al.; PIONEER Consortium / RANO resect. (2026).** A comprehensive framework for glioma surgery, part 2: perioperative recommendations for neurological, language, functional, and quality-of-life assessment. *The Lancet Oncology* 27:e24–e32. DOI: `10.1016/S1470-2045(25)00532-7`; PMID `41449155`.  
    Provides a contemporary adult framework for measuring the clinical outcomes that should matter in future molecularly guided surgical studies: neurological, language, functional and quality-of-life outcomes rather than EOR alone.

## Supporting perioperative surgical rationale

26. **Thompson EM, et al. (2018).** The clinical importance of medulloblastoma extent of resection: a systematic review. *Journal of Neuro-Oncology* 139:523–539. DOI: `10.1007/s11060-018-2906-5`  
    Systematic review showing uncertainty around the prognostic value of extent of resection in medulloblastoma and emphasizing the need to interpret surgical radicality in the molecular era.

## Explicit exclusions / emerging evidence

- **Euskirchen et al., Acta Neuropathologica 2017** — landmark same-day genomic/epigenomic nanopore CNS tumour paper, but outside the requested 2018–2026 window.
- medRxiv versions of Rapid-CNS2 and iSCORED — excluded because peer-reviewed journal versions are available.
- the 2025 medRxiv version of **How Sturgeon guides the surgeon** is superseded by the peer-reviewed *Neuro-Oncology* 2026 publication.
- 2025 Oslo *Neuro-Oncology* supplement abstract reporting >50% modulation of resection strategy remains conference-level evidence and is not treated as a core reference until a full peer-reviewed article is available.
